# Intent 0017 amendment 1: feats and their bonuses

Status: accepted
Amends: [intent 0017](0017-achievements-rework.md) outcome 7 (the small bonus) and the constraint "The bonuses stay
small"
Product owner: Zkalf (chose bigger bonuses for the grind on 2026-10-24)
Date: 2026-10-24

## Why
The sim shows that an average walker (5,000 steps a day) earns kill counts such as "10 Goblin Brutes" or "kill the
Young Dragon once" without any effort. The owner wants some achievements to cost a sacrifice: a long grind that the
player chooses. A grind of days needs a reward of the same size. "+1 Attack" is less than one level gives.

## Change

### Two kinds of achievement
1. A **milestone** comes on the way through the game. Its reward is a bigger step bank or an item.
2. A **feat** costs a sacrifice: a long grind of a monster after it pays well, or a long chase for luck.
3. Only a feat gives a stat bonus or the queue slot.

### The bonus of a feat
4. The bonus of a feat is as big as the grind. Outcome 7 no longer says "small".
5. The bonus grows with the stage of the feat. The spec sets the numbers. A start:

   | Stage | Grind at 5,000 steps a day | A feat gives one of these |
   |---|---|---|
   | Early | about 3 days | +4 Attack, +4 Defense or +40 HP |
   | Mid | about 7 days | +7 Attack, +7 Defense, +80 HP, +4 Crit or +4 Dodge |
   | Late | about 14 days | +10 Attack, +10 Defense, +130 HP, +6 Crit, +6 Hit or +6 Block |

6. The constraint "The bonuses stay small" becomes: **talents stay the main way to build a character.** A player
   without feats still makes good progress.

### Rewards and monsters
7. An item that a reward gives is never an item that a monster of the criterion drops.
8. An item that a reward gives is never made from the drops of a monster of the criterion.
9. An item that a reward gives is never the chest item of a spot in the criterion.

## Balance cost
A player with many feats is about 30% stronger in Attack at level 30 than a player without feats. The player pays
for this with weeks of grind. The sim checks that the pace of the average walker stays in its target.
